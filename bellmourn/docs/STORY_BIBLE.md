# BELLMOURN: Story Bible (PROPOSAL)

This is a proposal for the author to revise. It keeps everything already in the game (the city, the districts, the bosses,
the four wardens, the Haven, Act II) and fixes the problems found in `docs/STORY_REPORT.md`: wardens with no motives, the
"Bell Warden" enemy clash, killing the Hushed for currency, the missing bell-ringing moment and the thin ending. Every new
line of in-game text that comes from this document is marked PROPOSAL in the code.

## The world in one paragraph

Long ago the First Founder caught the voice of a dying god in bronze and cast the Great Bell of St. Orrin. Each dawn its
toll gives the world one more day; the city that grew around it, Bellmourn, kept a hundred lesser bells that echo the
great one. Bells here are not decoration: they are how the world keeps turning. Deep beneath and far beyond, an ancient
engine of rings and eyes, the Wheel, has always turned against the toll. Its priests, the Hollow Choir, teach the
gnostic heresy that the world is a prison the Founder built, and that only silence sets souls free.

## Backstory (before the game)

- One hundred nights ago, Saint Silence, once Orrin of the Wardens, first keeper of the Great Bell, heard the Hollow
  Choir's teaching and believed it. He stilled the bell with his own hand.
- Most of the Bell-Wardens followed him and swore the Oath of Silence. They still walk the city in their armour, shields
  raised: these are the "Bell Warden" foes. The heroes are the last wardens who refused the oath and were driven out.
- Without the toll the rain began and never stopped, and the night never ended. The people lost their voices: the
  Hushed. Their voices did not die. They sleep in them as sparks.
- The Pale Regent sold the dawn to the Choir in exchange for an endless ball on the Crown Heights ("Up here they paid for
  the night to stay").
- The Bellfounder, master of the foundry where Bram worked, poured a false bell for the Choir: the silent bell.

**Embers:** when a warden strikes a Hushed down, the voice sleeping in it is released as an ember. Embers are freed
voices; the wardens carry them back to the Haven fire, which is fed by them. Killing a Hushed frees it. (This replaces
"killing them for currency".)

**The Haven** is the last lamplighters' shrine: the only fire in the city lit from the Great Bell's own forge-flame,
which is why the night cannot touch it. (This replaces "the last fire in the city".)

## The wardens (playable)

Each has a reason to fight, a flaw, a relationship and a short line style.

1. **Carmine, the Bell-Warden** (blade). Orrin's last apprentice, exiled for refusing the oath. Comes home to ring the
   bell their master silenced. Flaw: still loves the man who betrayed them. Speaks little, plainly.
2. **Vesper, the Night Huntress** (crossbow). Hunted on the rooftops for a hundred nights; her younger brother Tam is one
   of the Hushed and she will not let anyone harm him. Flaw: trusts no one. Dry, quick lines.
3. **Bram, the Bellbreaker** (hammer). A foundry smith; he helped pour the silent bell without knowing what it was.
   Wants to break what he made. Flaw: guilt makes him reckless. Warm, heavy humour.
4. **Sister Ash, the Lamplighter** (lantern staff). Keeper of the Haven fire, last of the lamplighters; joins after
   Mother Tallow, who was her order's prioress, falls. Flaw: believes she must carry everyone. Gentle, certain.
5. **Kestrel, the Gaoler's Daughter** (twin knives). NEW. Locked in her father's gaol for trying to free the condemned.
   Freed when the Gaoler falls; fights to undo his work. Flaw: anger. Sharp, fast lines.
6. **Hollis, the Drowned Bellman** (anchor and chain). NEW. The harbour's bell-ringer, a Hushed whose voice came back when
   the Harbormaster fell; the first Hushed ever to speak again, and proof the Hushed can be saved. Flaw: half of him is
   still under the water. Slow, strange, kind lines.
7. **Mother Wren, the Choir-Witch** (sung staff of sound). NEW. Once the Drowned Deacon's choirmistress, she sang for
   the Choir until she understood what the songs did. Joins after the Deacon falls. Knows the Hollow Choir's secrets.
   Flaw: her old hymns still answer her. Ornate, sorrowful lines.

**Recruitment:** Carmine, Vesper and Bram start. Kestrel joins after the Gaoler, Sister Ash after Mother Tallow, Hollis
after the Harbormaster, and Mother Wren after the Deacon. Each joins in a short cutscene at the boss's arena or the next
Haven.

## Plot

### Prologue: The Old Road

A wet road, the city's silhouette with its dark belfry. Carmine walks alone; a crow-masked Bell Warden blocks the gate
and names them "oathbreaker". The training is Carmine fighting their way to the gate. At the gate Vesper drops from a
roof and Bram steps out of the dark with his hammer. Three wardens. "We ring it, or we drown."

### Act I: The City (eight districts)

- **Gallows Gate**, the Gaoler: "The keys stay with me." He falls; in his cell block they find Kestrel. She takes his
  keys and joins.
- **Lantern Row**, Mother Tallow, who keeps candles burning with stolen voices. She falls; Sister Ash, who sheltered the
  wardens at the Haven, comes to close her prioress's eyes, and joins.
- **Mirewater Docks**, the Harbormaster drags the drowned ashore to serve the Choir. He falls; the harbour bell rings
  once by itself and Hollis, a Hushed bell-ringer, speaks: "...I can hear again." The first Hushed to come back. Hope.
- **The Drowned Nave**, the Drowned Deacon's sermon is the tide. He falls; his choirmistress Mother Wren stops singing
  for the Choir and joins. She tells them what Silence did.
- **The Undercroft**, the Ossuary Knight, keeper of ten thousand names of the dead. He falls; the names on the walls
  begin to glow, the dead remembered.
- **The Bell Foundry**, the Bellfounder poured the silent bell. Bram faces his old master. "You taught me the bell is
  a promise." He falls; Bram breaks the false bell's mould.
- **The Crown Heights**, the Pale Regent, who sold the dawn. She reveals the Hollow Choir and the Wheel, and that
  Silence did it "out of love for all of you".
- **The Belfry**, Saint Silence. Before the fight he and Carmine speak (master and apprentice). After it, the
  bell-ringing scene: the player strikes the Great Bell (press attack three times). The bell rings. Dawn begins over the
  roofs, the Hushed lift their heads, Tam among them, and Vesper runs to him. Then the toll falters: a crack runs up the
  bell. Silence, dying: "One bell cannot outsing the Wheel. It heard you. Now it is coming." END OF ACT I.

### Act II: Beyond the Walls (four regions)

The toll carried beyond the walls and woke what the Wheel had left asleep. The wardens follow the Choir's road to its
source.

- **The Frostmarch**, the Hoarfrost King, whose breath stilled the pass so no one could carry the bell's song north.
- **The Ash Dunes**, Ashmaw, the wyrm beneath a city older than Bellmourn, the First Founder's own city, buried.
- **The Catacombs**, Abbot Morrow keeps the dead at prayer. Wren learns here what the Choir's last song is meant to do.
- **The Abyss**, the Hollow Archon, the Wheel's high priest. The revelation: the Great Bell holds the voice of the god
  who made the world. The Wheel is that god's jailer and wants it to finish dying; "then the prison ends, and every soul
  is free." The Archon falls but laughs: "You cannot stop a wheel by breaking a spoke." Above the Abyss the Wheel
  starts to turn. END OF ACT II.

### Act III: The Wheel (three regions)

- **The Weeping Marsh**: where the First Founder's foundry sank. The Bog Mother hoards drowned bells and their voices.
- **The Clockwork Spire**: the Wheel's machinery, gears the size of districts. The Clockmaster keeps the Wheel's time.
- **The Empyrean**: the hub of the Wheel, white and gold, a heaven turned inside out. At its heart, the Voice in the
  Bell, the dying god, guarded by the Wheel itself (the final boss).

### The choice and the endings

Before the last fight the Voice speaks to the warden: it is tired; it could die, ending the world gently; or be bound
again; or, if enough voices sing with it, be changed.

1. **Dawn (bind):** defeat the Wheel's guardian, then RING. The Voice is bound again; the world continues as it was. The
   wardens become the new keepers of the Great Bell. Bittersweet; the rain stops in Bellmourn.
2. **Silence (release):** choose SILENCE. The warden takes Saint Silence's place: the world winds down into a peaceful,
   endless night. The bad ending, played straight and quiet.
3. **The New Song (true ending):** requires all seven wardens recruited and every boss's Echo gathered. The option SING
   appears: the seven wardens and every freed voice sing with the Voice; it is not bound and does not die: it becomes the
   dawn. The Wheel stops for good, its eyes closing one by one. The rain ends everywhere. The last shot: the city
   ringing every one of its hundred bells.

Each ending has its own short cutscene, then the credits. After any ending, **New Game+** opens: keep levels and gear;
bosses return Ascended.

## Bosses with upgrades

- **Echoes:** each boss leaves an Echo, the voice it stole. Wearing an Echo gives a power drawn from that boss (for
  example the Gaoler's Echo: hits chain enemies in place for a moment). Echoes are worn in Echo slots at the Haven and
  are counted for the true ending.
- **Ascended bosses:** in New Game+ every boss returns Ascended: more health, new moves, a fourth phase, and a better
  Echo.

## Tone and style rules for all lines

- Short lines, at most two lines of the 5x7 font on a phone (about 40 characters each).
- No modern words. Bells, voices, lamps, rain and dawn are the recurring images.
- The Hushed are victims, never monsters. The wardens never call them "things".
- Every boss gets one line before its fight and one as it falls.

## Notes from building the scenes (PROPOSAL)

Gaps the bible left open, and how the cutscenes fill them for now (`js/game/scenes/`):

- **The lead is whoever the player chose.** The prologue is written for Carmine, but Vesper or Bram can be
  picked first. The scenes follow the chosen warden; the other two starters are the ones who join at the
  gate. The crow-masked warden calls only Carmine "oathbreaker".
- **Skipping the Old Road.** The wardens' meeting at the gate plays at the start of Gallows Gate instead.
- **Sister Ash keeps the Haven before she joins.** She is there at the first visit and stays by the fire;
  after Lantern Row she can be played.
- **Where the Hushed are when the bell rings.** Some followed the wardens up the tower stair; Tam is among
  them, so Vesper can reach him on the bell platform.
- **The wardens travel together.** A warden who is not the one being played still appears in the scenes
  that are about them (Bram at the Foundry, Vesper at the bell).
- **The choice.** RING and SING are followed by the fight with the Wheel; SILENCE ends the game at once
  (the Silence ending needs no fight). A choice made stays made if the warden falls to the Wheel.
- **Where the endings play.** The Dawn: the Belfry, the wardens kneeling as the bell's new keepers. Silence:
  the Belfry, the lamps going out one by one. The New Song: Gallows Gate at sunrise, where the game began,
  the freed Hushed singing and the city's bells ringing.
- **The Archon's line.** "You cannot stop a wheel by breaking a spoke" is spoken in his scene; his falling
  line in the fight is a different one, so it is not heard twice.
